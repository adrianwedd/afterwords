### B) SYNTHESIS SCRIPT

You’re looking at a paradox that defines the modern enterprise. Most large companies are rushing to fund AI initiatives, with global investment hitting hundreds of billions of dollars. Yet, only a tiny fraction consider themselves truly AI-mature. You see experiments everywhere, but few actually scale. The common reaction is to blame the technology or the execution, but that’s missing the point. The problem isn't technical. It’s economic.

To understand why, you have to look at the AI Productivity J-Curve. Economists like Erik Brynjolfsson have shown that transformative technologies follow a specific pattern. When you first adopt a general-purpose technology—like steam, electricity, or computing—you don't see an immediate spike in productivity. You actually see a dip. Measured productivity often drops because you’re pouring resources into the heavy, invisible work of transformation.

This is where most companies fail. They expect immediate returns, but they haven't yet built the intangible capital required for success. This means redesigning your business processes, fixing your data governance, and reskilling your entire workforce. You aren't just buying software; you are fundamentally changing how your organization operates. While you're in this trough, your dashboards look like you're going backward. You're spending capital on infrastructure that doesn't show up as immediate output.

This is exactly what leads to pilot purgatory. You launch a project, it works technically, but because you're evaluating it with a standard short-term ROI model, it looks like a failure. Management pulls the funding, the pilot dies, and you're stuck in a cycle of constant, low-impact experimentation. 

The secret is recognizing that the J-Curve is a multi-year investment, not a quarterly optimization. The most successful organizations follow a simple rule of thumb: seventy percent of your effort should go toward people and process redesign, twenty percent to data and infrastructure, and only ten percent to the algorithms themselves. If you are spending your budget in reverse, you aren't building a transformation; you're just paying for an experiment. To survive the trough and reach the upswing, you have to stop managing AI as a tech procurement and start managing it as an organizational overhaul.
