### B) SYNTHESIS SCRIPT

You are looking at the Afterglow Engine, a tool designed for what I call sonic archaeology. The premise is simple: your best sounds already exist, but they are buried in your finished work—hidden in the reverb tails of tracks you forgot, the sub-harmonics of a pad you used once, or the room tone of a recording you made years ago. 

Extracting these textures manually is slow, often killing the creative impulse that wanted them in the first place. That is where this engine comes in. It acts as a silent, patient studio assistant that walks through your archive to find these moments where the music stops moving and starts breathing. It doesn't create new tracks or make creative choices for you. Instead, it carefully unweaves your past work to isolate stable pads, textural grain, and subtle atmospheric dust. 

It stabilizes these findings and re-bottles them as distilled sonic pigments—exported as 44.1 kHz WAV files optimized for hardware samplers. It is essentially a way to grind down fragments of your own musical history and re-bottle them into fresh, reusable textures. 

When you use the Afterglow Engine, you are not just reaching for standard sample libraries; you are dipping into distilled memories of your own work. Future projects become painted with the ground-up archaeology of everything you have touched. If you want to start turning your archives into your new palette, head over to the project repository at github.com/adrianwedd/afterglow-engine.
