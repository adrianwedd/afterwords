### B) SYNTHESIS SCRIPT

When you are raising a neurodivergent child, you rarely receive a manual. Most parents find themselves navigating a dense fog of clinical terms, realizing that the standard parenting operating system simply does not boot on this hardware. That is why I built SPARK—a support partner for awareness, regulation, and kindness. 

SPARK is a robotics platform designed specifically for an AuDHD profile. It is not a tutor meant to optimize productivity, and it certainly is not a therapist designed to fix behavior. The most radical thing about SPARK is not the code; it is the philosophy. It rejects the traditional hierarchy where the human is the master and the machine is the servant. Instead, it exists as a non-coercive companion that adapts to the human.

For a child with pathological demand avoidance, a standard verbal command does not process as a simple instruction; it registers as a neurological threat. SPARK communicates in declarative language. Instead of telling a child to put on their shoes, SPARK might simply observe that the shoes are by the door. By leading with rapport, we avoid triggering the brain’s resistance.

We have to move beyond the myth that executive function is a matter of willpower. It is a finite physiological resource. SPARK acts as a prosthetic, providing the necessary scaffolding for a brain that may be running low on fuel. It manages routines and transitions not as moral obligations, but as external resources.

One of our most important features is silence. In a world of devices designed to beep and demand attention, SPARK knows how to be quiet. When a child is in the middle of an amygdala hijack, they cannot use logic to navigate the distress. During these moments, SPARK initiates a protocol of safety, silence, and space. It stops moving and stays present, acting as a calm fixture for co-regulation.

Finally, we navigate the interest-based nervous system through what I call sideways engagement. If a task needs initiation, the robot might share a fascinating fact to spark curiosity rather than addressing the child directly, framing transitions as puzzles rather than chores. 

By utilizing a three-brain architecture—handling reactive interaction, autonomic presence, and cognitive reflection—SPARK feels like a companion rather than a tool. You can find the full source code and documentation at github.com/adrianwedd/spark.
