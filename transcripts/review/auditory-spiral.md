### B) SYNTHESIS SCRIPT

You are looking at the history of a nocturnal institution. Auditory Spiral was an overnight electronic music programme on RTRFM 92.1 in Perth, broadcasting from midnight to 6 AM on Sunday nights. During the 1990s and into the 2000s, this six-hour block was an immersion in minimal techno, dark ambient, industrial, and experimental electronic music. At a time when these sounds were virtually inaccessible in Western Australia, the show provided a vital bridge to the global electronic underground.

The programme was driven by Shamus and Nicolai, who curated hypnotic, continuous journeys through these challenging genres, with Roger helping manage the demanding overnight schedule. Because the station had no permanent DJ equipment, the presenters would carry their own turntables, mixers, and crates of records to the booth each week, essentially building a temporary club environment at midnight and breaking it all down at dawn.

The six-hour format allowed for long-form live mixing, where twelve-minute instrumental tracks could unfold without the commercial pressures found in daytime radio. This show existed in symbiosis with a local culture of illicit warehouse raves and improvised venues, serving as the Sunday night anchor for a broader matrix of nocturnal programming that included shows like Beats Per Minute, Looney Tunes, and Rhythm Trippin.

Because many of the tracks played were obscure white-label imports or limited European pressings, listeners taped the broadcasts directly off the radio. These home-recorded cassettes were dubbed, traded, and circulated through local record shops and schools, forming a thriving cassette network. In effect, these broadcasts functioned as an open-source audio syllabus, educating a generation of local DJs, promoters, and producers.

Auditory Spiral was the uncompromising nocturnal laboratory where the most challenging fringes of dance music were explored, building a community around shared, difficult, and beautiful sounds. You can explore the full story behind the programme at the link provided on the site.
